## When and where
This message covers Alabama for the 48 hours from 7:00 p.m. CDT Thursday, October 8, to 7:00 p.m. CDT Saturday, October 10. Flood Watches are in effect for all Alabama counties, with start times ranging from Friday afternoon in south Alabama to late Friday night in north Alabama.

## Flood risk
The greatest rainfall-flood risk is in south Alabama, especially near and east of Isaias's track. Using the current WPC Moderate-or-higher excessive rainfall area, the highest-risk Alabama counties are Baldwin, Escambia, Conecuh, Monroe, Geneva, Clarke, Covington, Mobile, Houston, Washington, and Wilcox. About 935,600 people live in these counties, including about 124,800 estimated in the FEMA 100-year floodplain. Escambia, Clarke, Mobile, and Wilcox also have high social vulnerability.

## Expected rainfall
South Alabama: widespread 3 to 10 inches, with local totals up to 15 inches possible where bands train. Central Alabama: 1.5 to 4 inches, locally up to 5 inches. North Alabama: broad areas of 3 to 4 inches, with isolated 5 to 7 inch totals possible.

## Uncertainty
The main uncertainty is where the heaviest rain bands set up. A band east of the center could focus the worst flooding over coastal Alabama and the western Florida Panhandle, or farther east toward the Florida Big Bend.

## What to do
Never drive through flooded roads: Turn Around, Don't Drown. Know your route to higher ground, or have a plan and supplies if you need to stay put. Monitor the National Weather Service and your county emergency management agency, and follow evacuation orders from local officials.
