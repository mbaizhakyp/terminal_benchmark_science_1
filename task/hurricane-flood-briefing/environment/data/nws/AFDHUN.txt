.SHORT TERM...
(Friday through Sunday)
Issued at 1107 AM CDT Thu Oct 8 2026

Attention rapidly turns southward as we head into Friday. 
Hurricane Isaias is currently forecast to make landfall somewhere 
along the MS/AL/FL coastline. The TN Valley will see clouds 
streaming in on Friday, particularly in Northeast AL, and the 
increased pressure gradient will cause winds to occasionally gust 
up to 20-25 mph, particularly heading into Friday evening. Cloud 
cover will result in a pretty stark difference in high 
temperatures Friday, with Northwest AL again peaking in the upper 
80s and those under persistent clouds sticking closer to 80, if 
not the upper 70s. Other features of focus will be the longwave 
trough to our west that will have a shortwave taking on a negative
tilt this weekend as Isaias approaches.

For now, we do not anticipate the initial rain bands to move in
until late Friday evening into the overnight period. This is where
the specific landfall location of Isaias can bring fairly
significant changes in impacts and locations. From here on out,
I'll break the AFD into specific tropical threats in order of
concern for our area at this point in the forecast process.

Wind: Isaias will be rapidly progressing inland on Saturday. As 
it moves over or near the TN Valley, we anticipate wind gusts up 
to 50 mph area-wide. High terrain regions of northeast AL will be 
particularly vulnerable. The latest forecast has moved the path of
the center of Isaias from northwest AL into northeast MS, which
will bring the more intense wind field on the east side of the
system over more of the TN Valley. The plan is for a wind-related
headline to be out for the whole area later this afternoon after
coordination with several other offices.

Tornadoes: Due to the slight westward shift of the track, more of
the rain bands will progress over our area. The tornado threat
would begin early Saturday morning when the first bands approach.
This threat would continue through Saturday evening until the
remnants of Isaias progress out of the area. The fly in this
ointment is as the approaching shortwave trough phases with
Isaias. There is a low chance that this may stall over the TN
Valley, which would prolong the tornado threat. There is a medium
to high chance at least portions of our area will be in a tornado
watch on Saturday.

Flooding: Tropical systems bring intense rain rates, and north-
south oriented rain bands will result in wide variability in rain
totals across the TN Valley. Broad swaths up to 3-4" are in the
current forecast with a low chance of high end swaths up to 5-7"
in areas that see multiple tropical bands. A flood watch for flash
flooding has been issued that encompasses this threat, please see
that for more details.

Issued at 922 PM CDT Wed Oct 7 2026

In wake of the system, winds will subside Sunday into Sunday

part of next week. This will again result in beautiful autumn
weather across the region for mid October. With ample sunshine and
dry conditions highs will return to the 80s and overnight lows
drop into the 50s.
